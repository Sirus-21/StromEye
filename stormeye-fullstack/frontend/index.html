Inputs

One satellite lies to you sometimes. Five don't.

No single sensor sees the whole storm. The model fuses these feeds so gaps in one get filled by another — tap a source to see what it actually contributes.

IR

Infrared brightness temp

Cloud-top temperature, day or night. The core signal for spotting an eye.

WV

Water vapor channel

Mid-to-upper atmosphere moisture — flags the environment a storm can feed on.

MW

Passive microwave

Sees through the cloud shield to the storm's actual structure underneath.

SCAT

Scatterometer winds

Ocean-surface wind vectors from radar backscatter — ground-truth for intensity.

VIS

Visible imagery

Daytime texture and banding detail that sharpens the eye-detection step.

Why infrared carries the most weight

It's the only channel available at every timestep, day and night, on a consistent schedule — so it anchors the time series the model learns from. Every other source corrects it in a different way: microwave reveals the real eyewall when cloud tops hide it, water vapor flags whether the storm has fuel, and scatterometer winds keep the intensity estimate honest.

Architecture

From raw swath to storm warning, six stages

Each stage hands off a cleaner, more structured version of the storm to the next.

01

Ingest & co-register

Pull concurrent passes from each satellite source and align them to a common grid and timestamp, so every pixel across sensors refers to the same patch of ocean.

geolocation matchingtemporal alignment
02

Clean & normalize

Mask stray clouds and sensor noise, correct calibration drift between satellites, and normalize brightness temperature and wind fields to a shared scale.

cloud maskingcalibration correction
03

Detect candidate systems

A CNN scans each frame for the spiral banding and closed circulation that separate an organizing cyclone from ordinary tropical convection.

CNN feature extractioncandidate flagging
04

Classify intensity

Structural features — eye clarity, symmetry, convective banding — map onto a Dvorak-informed intensity estimate and Saffir-Simpson category.

Dvorak-informed scoringcategory assignment
05

Model the sequence

A ConvLSTM / temporal transformer reads the last 24–48 hours of frames to learn how this storm's structure has been evolving.

ConvLSTMtemporal attention
06

Predict track & intensity

Outputs a forecast cone for the storm's path and a probability curve for intensification over the next 6–72 hours.

track forecastintensification probability
